# Revenue analytics

The administrator's `/app/analytics` page reports booked sales (fare plus tax, less discounts), not cash collections, commission income, or profit. Cancelled bookings and future booking dates are excluded. Client booking requests contribute no sales until accepted into bookings. Existing financial rules continue to apply.

Choose booking-date months to analyze when sales were recorded, or departure-date months to analyze travel demand. Departure mode includes already booked future trips, clearly identified as scheduled departures rather than forecasts. Year, country/area, and calendar-period filters affect every total, ranking, chart, and export. March–May is the northern-hemisphere spring preset; December is the Christmas-period preset. These are calendar groupings, not universal travel seasons or proven recurring peaks.

The page includes monthly sales and a values table, country rankings and shares, ties in leading countries/months, a calendar-month view, service/category totals, and monthly/country CSV exports. All-years mode shows recorded months; selecting a year includes zero-sales months (future booking-date months are omitted). The current booking month is identified as partial. Seasonal results reflect available history and do not automatically establish seasonality.

Packages and bookings now store a reporting country code. Acceptance copies the package country into the confirmed booking, and direct booking creation also saves a snapshot. Subsequent package changes do not change existing bookings' countries. Administrators can correct country metadata on paid bookings while their fare/customer protections remain enforced.

Existing records are preserved. When a booking lacks a country, an exact country-name destination or an explicit `city, country` suffix can identify it without city geocoding. Ambiguous or multi-country destinations remain unspecified. Unclassified sales remain in totals and CSVs but do not compete for the leading-country title or ranked list; the UI shows a classification notice. Set countries on legacy bookings to improve coverage. Package editing does not retroactively reclassify bookings.

Country/area choices use codes from [UN M49](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/methodology/m49/overview/), plus TW, with English labels from `Intl.DisplayNames`. The server validates country codes and all reporting filters. The new endpoint is administrator-only and respects the existing authentication and verification gates. The two added columns retain private-schema grants and RLS; no public analytics endpoint is introduced.

Verification covers centavo totals, country/month reconciliation, cancellations, future sales exclusion, date-basis differences, spring/December filters, empty results, ties, partial/scheduled months, country snapshots, paid-record metadata corrections, protected fares, invalid filters, and client/anonymous rejection. PostgreSQL integration also exercises country persistence and the analytics endpoint. Browser fixtures are isolated fictional records and never populate production.

Browser verification confirmed that a fictional October-booked Japan trip appears under December departures, and the monthly CSV exports the same PHP 60,000 total. The 390px layout had no horizontal overflow. A package created through the custom country dropdown persisted `JP` and its exact centavo price. Builds and 41 automated checks passed; one existing opt-in remote financial-concurrency check remained skipped. Supabase advisors reported no issues after the country-column migration.
